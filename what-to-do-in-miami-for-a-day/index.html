What to Do in Miami for a Day: an Hour-by-Hour Plan

One day is enough to feel Miami if you stop trying to see all of it. The plan below is built around one rule: stay on the beach side in the morning, cross to the mainland once in the early afternoon, and come back once for sunset. Everything else follows from that.

The short version

7:30 AM beach walk and Art Deco on Ocean Drive. 10:00 AM a 1-hour or 2-hour self-guided jet ski ride on Biscayne Bay, 15 minutes north of South Beach. 1:00 PM lunch and a walk in Little Havana. 3:30 PM Wynwood murals. 6:00 PM sunset at South Pointe Park. 8:00 PM dinner in Brickell or on the Miami River. Rideshare between stops.

7:30 AM: South Beach and the Art Deco district

Direct answer: start on the sand. South Beach is public and nearly empty before 9 AM, and the pastel Art Deco hotels on Ocean Drive photograph best in low morning light. Walk the beach from about 5th Street north, come back along Ocean Drive, then cut over to Collins for coffee. Budget 90 minutes. Do not drive here if you can avoid it; parking is the single biggest time-waster in Miami Beach.

10:00 AM: 1–2 hours on Biscayne Bay by jet ski

Direct answer: late morning is the best window for the water. The bay is usually calmer and there is less boat traffic than in the afternoon, and in summer you avoid the storms that can roll in after 2 PM. Jet Ski Miami 7 is in the Miami Beach / North Bay Village area, about 15 minutes from South Beach by rideshare, open daily 7 AM – 6 PM.

How to fit it in:

  • Book a 10:00 AM slot and arrive at 9:30 AM. The 30 minutes before the ride cover ID check, the safety briefing and the route overview.
  • A 1-hour ride ($189 per jet ski, up to 2 people, +$30 for a passenger) covers the Venetian Islands (10–15 minutes away) and Star Island (15–20 minutes) and gets you back by 11:00.
  • A 2-hour ride ($349 per jet ski, with a bonus 30 minutes, so 2.5 hours on the water) adds the Haulover sandbar, a 25–30 minute ride north, with a swim stop. You would be back around 12:30 PM. The sandbar route is described on the Haulover sandbar page.

The ride is self-guided: nobody rides behind you, but the riding zone is GPS-monitored and the jet skis slow down automatically in no-wake zones, so you do not have to memorize the markers. Fuel, life vests and the briefing are included. A $30 booking deposit holds the slot; the balance is paid at check-in, plus a $150 refundable security hold per jet ski on a card, released about 5 business days after return. Full pricing is on the price page; booking is at /book.

One rule that trips people up: anyone born on or after January 1, 1988 who drives needs a Florida boater safety card. The temporary certificate takes about 30 minutes online and costs about $14; the link is sent after booking, and you can do it the night before from your hotel. Details on the license page. The person who books and signs must be 21+ with a government photo ID, drivers must be 14+, and there is no alcohol before riding.

1:00 PM: lunch and Calle Ocho in Little Havana

From the jet ski location, Little Havana is a 25–35 minute rideshare across the bay. Eat first (Cuban sandwiches, roast pork, a cafecito from a walk-up window), then walk Calle Ocho between about 13th and 17th Avenues: Domino Park, cigar rollers, fruit stands, music spilling out of doorways. Ninety minutes is plenty.

3:30 PM: Wynwood

Wynwood is a 15-minute ride north of Little Havana. The ticketed Wynwood Walls is the curated core; the surrounding blocks are covered in free murals, with galleries, breweries and coffee shops in between. Weekday afternoons are quieter than weekends. If you have energy left, the Design District is a short hop further north.

6:00 PM: sunset at South Pointe Park

Cross back to Miami Beach (20–25 minutes from Wynwood). South Pointe Park is at the southern tip of South Beach, with a long pier and a clear view west over the bay. Cruise ships leave through Government Cut in the late afternoon and pass right next to the pier. Sunset timing varies a lot by season, so check it that morning.

8:00 PM: dinner

Three honest options. Brickell for a skyline view and a modern menu; the Miami River for waterfront tables between the boatyards; South Beach a block or two inland from Ocean Drive, where prices drop and quality usually rises. Then either South Beach or Wynwood for a drink, depending on where you are sleeping.

What to bring

  • Swimwear under your clothes, a towel and a change of top. You will get wet on the jet ski.
  • Sunscreen, sunglasses with a strap, a hat for the afternoon walking.
  • Government photo ID for the person who booked (21+), and the boater card confirmation on the phone for drivers born on or after January 1, 1988.
  • A card for the jet ski balance and the $150 security hold (card, Apple Pay and Google Pay are accepted at check-in).
  • A waterproof phone pouch if you want photos on the water.
  • Comfortable shoes for Little Havana and Wynwood; flip-flops are fine for the morning.

If the weather turns

Summer afternoons can bring short, heavy storms; winter mornings can be cool. Keeping the water in the late-morning slot is the best protection. If conditions are unsafe, Jet Ski Miami 7 cancels and either refunds the unused time including the deposit or reschedules for free. If you need to cancel yourself, do it at least 48 hours before and the ride moves once to another date within 90 days; the booking deposit itself is non-refundable. Museums (PAMM and Frost Science, side by side in Downtown) are the standard rainy-afternoon swap.

Variations

  • Family with kids: keep the 1-hour ride, swap Wynwood for the Frost Science museum, and have dinner early on Miami Beach.
  • Couples: book the 2-hour ride for the sandbar swim stop and the extra 30 minutes; move sunset to a Brickell rooftop.
  • Cruise passengers: see the jet ski before your cruise page for PortMiami timing.
  • Big group: up to 6 jet skis per booking; the bachelor party page covers deposits and splitting time.

Frequently asked questions

Can you see Miami in one day?

Yes, if you plan by geography: beach and Art Deco in the morning, Biscayne Bay before noon, Little Havana and Wynwood in the afternoon, sunset back on the beach. Skip the Everglades and Key Biscayne on a one-day visit.

How long should a jet ski ride be on a one-day trip?

One hour is enough for the Venetian Islands and Star Island loop; two hours (2.5 hours on the water with the bonus 30 minutes) adds a swim stop at the Haulover sandbar. Both fit a late-morning window.

What time should I start my day in Miami?

Around 7:30 AM. The beach is empty, the bay is calmest in the morning, and you will be done with the water before the midday heat and any summer afternoon storms.

How far is the jet ski location from South Beach?

About 15 minutes by car or rideshare. From Downtown or Brickell it is 20–25 minutes, from Bal Harbour or Surfside about 10 minutes. The exact meeting point is sent right after booking.

What do I need to bring for the jet ski part of the day?

Swimwear worn under your clothes, a government photo ID for the person who booked (21+), the boater card confirmation on your phone if you were born on or after January 1, 1988, sunscreen, a towel and a card for the balance and the security hold.

Is a boat tour or a jet ski better for a one-day visit?

A narrated boat tour from Bayside is easier and cheaper per person and needs no card. A self-guided jet ski is faster, lets you choose the route and includes a sandbar swim stop on a 2-hour ride.
