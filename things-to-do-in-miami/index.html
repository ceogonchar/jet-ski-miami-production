Things to Do in Miami (2026): 15 Ideas by Neighborhood

Miami is several cities next to each other: the beach side, the mainland core (Downtown, Brickell, Wynwood, Little Havana), the islands and the water that connects them, Biscayne Bay. A good trip mixes at least two. Below are 15 ideas grouped by area, then a 1-day and a 3-day plan you can copy.

Quick answer: what should you do in Miami?

If you have one day, see South Beach and the Art Deco district in the morning, get on Biscayne Bay around midday (a boat tour from Bayside or a self-guided jet ski ride), spend the afternoon in Wynwood or Little Havana, and finish with sunset at South Pointe Park. With three days, add Vizcaya, the Downtown museums, Key Biscayne and an Everglades half-day. Plan by geography: the causeways take 15–25 minutes each way, and crossing them more than twice a day eats your afternoon.

Miami Beach

1. South Beach and Ocean Drive

The classic. Ocean Drive is the strip of pastel Art Deco hotels facing Lummus Park and the beach. The beach is public and free; you pay only for chairs and umbrellas. Go before 10 AM for an empty beach. Ocean Drive restaurants are priced for tourists; a block inland is cheaper.

2. Art Deco walk

The Art Deco Historic District is free to walk. A self-guided loop from 5th Street to about 15th Street along Ocean Drive, then back along Collins, covers the highlights in an hour.

3. Sunset at South Pointe Park

South Pointe Park is the southern tip of South Beach, where the shipping channel into PortMiami meets the ocean. It has a long pier and a clear view west over the bay; cruise ships leave through the cut in the late afternoon. Free and a short walk from Ocean Drive.

4. Haulover Park and the Haulover sandbar

Haulover Park is a county park at the north end of Miami Beach with a wide ocean beach and the Haulover Inlet, where boats pass between the bay and the Atlantic. On the bay side is the Haulover sandbar, a shallow area where boats and jet skis stop to swim. You reach it only by water; our guide to riding to the Haulover sandbar explains the route.

Mainland: Wynwood, Little Havana, Downtown, Design District

5. Wynwood Walls and the mural blocks

Miami's street-art district. Wynwood Walls is a ticketed, curated mural park; the surrounding blocks are covered in free murals, galleries, breweries and coffee shops. Weekdays are quieter. Allow two hours.

6. Little Havana and Calle Ocho

Calle Ocho (SW 8th Street) is the heart of Cuban Miami: cafés serving cafecito through a walk-up window, cigar shops, Domino Park, fruit stands and live music in the evenings.

7. Design District

A compact, walkable area of luxury boutiques, public art and design showrooms north of Wynwood. Worth an hour even if you are not shopping; pairs with Wynwood.

8. Pérez Art Museum Miami and Frost Science

Two ticketed museums side by side in Downtown's waterfront park: PAMM for contemporary art with a terrace over the bay, Frost Science for the planetarium and aquarium. The best rainy-day or hot-afternoon option.

9. Bayside Marketplace and boat tours

Bayside is the Downtown waterfront mall where most sightseeing boat tours depart. To see the bay without driving anything yourself, this is the easiest option: ticketed, narrated, about an hour and a half along Star Island and the Venetian Islands.

10. Miami River dining

The riverbanks between Biscayne Bay and Downtown have turned into a strip of waterfront restaurants between the boatyards. Good for dinner after a Downtown day.

11. Vizcaya Museum and Gardens

An early-20th-century estate on the bay in Coconut Grove with formal gardens and a stone barge breakwater. Ticketed, quiet and photogenic; half a day with lunch in the Grove.

Islands and the bay

12. Key Biscayne and Cape Florida

Over the Rickenbacker Causeway are Crandon Park and Bill Baggs Cape Florida State Park: calmer beaches, bike paths and the Cape Florida lighthouse, with a parking fee. The best beach day if South Beach feels too busy.

13. Jet ski on Biscayne Bay (self-guided)

14. Everglades day trip

The Everglades begin roughly 45 minutes west of the city; airboat rides and the national park's boardwalk trails are the usual format. Allow a half-day including driving.

15. Nightlife

Three different scenes: Brickell rooftops for a drink with a skyline view, Wynwood for a casual bar crawl, South Beach for the big-name clubs. Rideshare between them.

Sample 1-day itinerary

  • 7:30 AM: coffee and a walk on South Beach while it is empty; Art Deco loop on Ocean Drive and Collins.
  • 10:00 AM: 1-hour or 2-hour self-guided jet ski ride on Biscayne Bay, 15 minutes north of South Beach. Arrive 30 minutes early for the briefing; morning water is calmest.
  • 1:00 PM: lunch in Little Havana, then Calle Ocho on foot.
  • 3:30 PM: Wynwood murals and a coffee or a brewery.
  • 6:00 PM: back to Miami Beach for sunset at South Pointe Park.
  • 8:00 PM: dinner on the Miami River or in Brickell.

Sample 3-day itinerary

  • Day 1 (beach side): South Beach, Art Deco walk, afternoon beach, South Pointe sunset, South Beach evening.
  • Day 2 (water and mainland): morning jet ski or Bayside boat tour, Downtown museums in the heat of the afternoon, Miami River dinner.
  • Day 3 (islands or west): Key Biscayne, or an Everglades half-day, then Wynwood and the Design District, Little Havana for dinner.

Practical notes

Use rideshare unless you have a parking plan; South Beach parking is the biggest time-waster for visitors. The bay is best in the morning and summer afternoons can bring short storms, so put water first and museums second. Jet ski drivers born on or after January 1, 1988 need a Florida boater safety card, done online in about 30 minutes; see whether you need a license to jet ski in Florida. Frequently asked questions

What is the best thing to do in Miami on a first visit?

Walk South Beach and the Art Deco district in the morning, get on Biscayne Bay around midday, and spend the afternoon in Wynwood or Little Havana. That one day covers the beach, the water and the culture.

Which things to do in Miami are free?

Public beaches (South Beach, North Beach, Haulover Park beach), the Art Deco district streets, Wynwood street murals outside the ticketed Walls, Calle Ocho, the Design District sidewalks and South Pointe Park are all free to walk.

How many days do you need in Miami?

Two to three days is enough for the main neighborhoods and one water activity. One full day works if you plan by geography and avoid crossing the causeways more than twice.

Is Biscayne Bay worth seeing from the water?

Yes. The skyline, the Venetian and Star Island homes and the Haulover sandbar are only visible from the water. Options range from a boat tour at Bayside to a self-guided jet ski rental from $189/hour.

What should I avoid doing in Miami?

Avoid trying to see Miami Beach, Downtown and the Everglades on the same day, driving to South Beach without a parking plan, and booking afternoon water activities in summer without a weather backup.

When is the best time of day for the water in Miami?

Mornings. The bay is usually calmer and there is less boat traffic. Summer afternoons can bring storms, so water plans are safest before 2 PM.

Do I need a license to rent a jet ski in Miami?

Anyone born on or after January 1, 1988 who drives needs a Florida boater safety card. The temporary certificate takes about 30 minutes online and costs about $14.
